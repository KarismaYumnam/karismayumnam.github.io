Drone-sonde and ground sensing-based evaluation of wind machines effectiveness in sweet cherry spring frost mitigation

ASABE 2023 Annual International Meeting, Omaha, NE, July 9-12, 2023 (Oral Presentation, Abstract ID:2301395), 2023

Authors/Contributors: S. Gorthi, B. Amogi, G.-A. Hoheisel, and L. R. Khot

Perennial specialty fruit crop in the Pacific Northwest (PNW) are susceptible to frost damage. When the bud experienced freezing temperatures below the critical temperatures, water inside the plant tissue freezes resulting in cold damage. Wind machines, which mixes the warm air at 15 to 25 metres above ground level (AGL) with the cold air at the ground surface during nocturnal inversions, are widely used by the growers to protect cold damage. However, the effectiveness of wind machines depends on the strength of temperature inversions and varies spatially within the orchard. Quantification of the effectiveness of wind machines with different inversion strengths and distances away from the wind machine is limited for the PNW region, USA. Therefore, the first objective of this study is to evaluate the effectiveness of wind machines in mixing the warm and cold air for different inversion strengths in sweet cherry orchard. The second objective is to map the temperature inversion strengths for different distances away from the wind machine when it is operating. Two MX2301A (accuracy: ±0.2℃, resolution: 0.01℃) temperature sensor were installed at 1.5 m and 9 m on the wind machine to monitor in-orchard temperature inversions. A gater equipped with MX2301A temperature sensor at every 1 m upto 9m above ground level will be parked inside the orchad at 20 m, 40 m and 60 m away from the wind machine during radiative frost nights to monitor the mixing of warm and cold air. Simultanous mapping of open-field (0 to 40 m above ground level) and in-orchard vertical temperature gradients (0 to 40 m above ground level at 20 m, 40 m, and 60 m away from wind machine) will be conducted using two drones equipped with iMET-XQ2 (accuracy: ±0.3 ℃, resolution: 0.01℃) temperature sensor when the wind machine is operating. Pertinent results was shared at the ASABE conference
